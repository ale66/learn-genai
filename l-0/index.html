General Introduction

Intelligence

Hard to define, relatively easy to recognise.

the Reaction/Though/reasoning triad

Reaction

  • automated

  • peripheral

  • no need to be awake

Thought

  • spontaneous

  • undirected

  • must be being awake (mostly)

Reasoning

  • intentional

  • directed: there’s a goal

  • explainable (legal arguments)

  • verbal

A Working definition:

intelligent behaviour:

adequate interaction with the context

intelligent behaviour

  • observable

  • active: something happens

. . .

Lying on the beach, gazing a beautiful scenery and thinking great thoughts ain’t behaviour.

Writing a poem about it (and getting it published) is intelligent behaviour.

Artificial

Intelligent behaviour (IB) from artifacts, hardware/software.

In this module: IB from software processes that handle/exchange text

Agent: software process that can operate on computers human-like

. . .

However,

  • in nature IB is related to motility (no veggies)

  • only embodied agents can hope to achieve IB

Artificial Intelligence

A branch of Computer Science

influx from Robotics, Psychology, Physiology, Philosophy (Logic), Maths (Stats) and whatnot

the term was coined at the 1956 Dartmouth Conference

build machines that behave intelligently

those are agents because they act in the world around them

The Dartmouth Schism

John McCarthy and Marvin Minsky disagreed

  • on how a mind actually works

  • hence, on how to build one

McCarthy

McCarthy

Observe IB in nature, understand its underlying principles

replicate it with the language of Maths and CS

Example

The flying of birds:

  • not really replicated, from Prometheus on

  • the physics is now well understood: flying by airplane is now possible

McCarthy’s vision

Every aspect of intelligence could eventually be described precisely enough to be programmed

Symbolic (variable), rule-based reasoning (logical inference)

if temperature > 30:
  tomatoes.cover()
  elif temperature > 20:
    tomatoes.water(10)

An abstract statement

the temperature variable makes it communicate with the context

Examples: knowledge graphs, automated planners, theorem provers, semantic web

Conclusions

McCarthy sees mind as a reasoning engine: it manipulates explicit, checkable statements about the world to determine what is true and what to do next.

Reading (Stanford page): - Concepts of Logical AI, 2000 - The little thoughts of thinking machines

Minsky

Cybernetics

Observe the source of IB in nature: the brain, understand its underlying principles

replicate it with the language of Maths and CS

focus on the specific experience of thought:

  • K-lines mental triggers that reactivate a useful past mental state, supporting memory and learning by analogy.
  • Emotions as a form of thought
  • No central commander thinking emerges bottom-up from agent interactions

Example

Optical neuro-motor control to follow objects

A software artifact, the neural network, shows evolutionary ability against external pressure

Minsky’s vision

The mind is not one thing but a society of small, simple processes that cooperate and compete to produce the emergent thought

IB emerges from the interaction of many ‘mindless’ parts.

no single trick behind intelligence — ‘the trick is that there is no trick.’

Examples: multi-agent systems, mixture-of-experts models, chain-of-thought prompting

Readings

  • The society of Mind, 1986

  • A Framework for Representing Knowledge, 1974

Summary and comparison

McCarthy Minsky
What is a mind? A reasoning engine A society of agents
Knowledge format Explicit logical sentences Frames, K-lines, loosely structured
Strength Precision, generality, provability Flexibility, handles exceptions naturally
Risk Brittle when world doesn’t fit the rules Hard to formalize, harder to guarantee correctness
Famous output Lisp, non-monotonic reasoning Frames, Society of Mind, early neural net research

Approaches to text: McCarthy

  • encode the grammar of natural language into software

  • position and punctuation guide understanding of the phrase

  • use it to generate correct phrases

  • software keeps internal model of the world, it can check what phrase is true (sometimes)

Example: la vecchia porta la sbarra

Approaches to text: Minsky

  • we learn the meaning of a word by its context

  • text is a bag of words

  • frequent co-occurrences create meaning

Modern Natural Language processing is Stats/Big Data

Today

Minsky won

  • prediction, e.g., value estimation, clustering etc. is done with approximate methods

  • many such methods are based on artificial neural networks: a software that mimics the neuro-electric working of neurons

  • AI by now is identified with approximate methods that learn from data

AI as a misnomer

  • predicting the temperature tomorrow is not AI, it’s Physics

  • yet, predicting temps. by a ‘neural net’ software that is trained on past data is AI

The ‘neural net’ replication captures both the accuracy and the inexplicability of humans feeling the weather

This may or may not be acceptable: Explicable AI

Example

Football matches are hard to predict, surprises are always possible

experts will be guessing, mostly

Yet, experts are often accurate in predicting the long-term success of teams, but not in a certain, numerical way

Consequences

Today Natural Language Processing (NLP) systems learn from text

text is data, input and output

Methods are inspired by neural net models

access to the statistical distribution of words

no access to the ground truth: the sun raises from East is just as good as the sun raises from West
